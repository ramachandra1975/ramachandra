Aesthetics of Society

Ancient Knowledge
for Modern Healing

Naredla Rama Chandra is an independent researcher, author, and cultural scholar based in Hyderabad, India. Over a distinguished career spanning multiple domains, he has devoted himself to excavating the scientific foundations embedded within India's ancient arts, scriptures, and metaphysical traditions.

His work bridges the Sapta Swaras of Carnatic music, the 108 Karanas of sacred dance, Beeja Aksharas of Sanskrit, and the living traditions of Yoga and Vedanta — interpreting them through the lens of modern bio-acoustics, neuroscience, and astrophysics.

The crowning vision of his research is the "Master Consciousness Protocol" — a systematic framework for natural healing and the liberation of human potential, derived entirely from India's eternal wisdom traditions.

Scholarly Context

Textual & Contextual References

Each publication is grounded in the primary source traditions of Indian knowledge systems. Below are the principal scriptural and scholarly works that underpin the research.

I

Complete Science of Consciousness

Primary Textual Sources

Rooted in the Mandukya Upanishad (states of consciousness: Jagrat, Svapna, Sushupti, Turiya), the Yoga Vasishtha (consciousness as the substratum of reality), and Adi Shankaracharya's Vivekachudamani. Cross-referenced with contemporary works by Sri Aurobindo (The Life Divine) and modern cognitive science literature on neural correlates of consciousness.

Contextual Note: This work directly challenges the reductionist computational model of consciousness, arguing that Indian epistemology provides a more complete account of awareness than Western physicalist frameworks.
II

Master Reference Compendium

Methodological Sources

Draws from Panini's Ashtadhyayi (Sanskrit grammar), Bharata Muni's Natya Shastra, Sarngadeva's Sangita Ratnakara (13th c. musicological treatise), and the Brihat Samhita of Varahamihira. Incorporates modern research standards from neuroscience, ethnomusicology, and cultural anthropology as comparative frameworks.

Contextual Note: Serves as the master index for all publications in the series, providing unified citation standards that bridge Sanskrit scholarly conventions and Western academic formats.
III

Sapta Swaras & Healing

Musicological & Medical References

Primary source is Sarngadeva's Sangita Ratnakara and the ancient Gandharva Veda (the Upaveda of music). References specific raga-roga (melody-disease) correspondences found in texts like Svararnava and Raga Nidhi. Cross-referenced with modern music therapy research and the work of Dr. T.V. Sairam on music and health in the Indian tradition.

Contextual Note: The seven swaras are not merely musical notes but are described in Vedic literature as manifestations of Nada Brahman — the primordial sound of creation — each carrying specific physiological resonance frequencies.
IV

108 Karanas & Beeja Aksharas

Dance, Sound & Cosmic Science

Primary source: Bharata Muni's Natya Shastra (Chapters IV, XIII) which codifies the 108 Karanas (combined hand-foot positions). Cross-referenced with Abhinaya Darpana of Nandikesvara and the Tantrarajatantra for Beeja Akshara correspondences. Astrophysical references drawn from Varahamihira's Brihat Jataka and modern planetary frequency research.

Contextual Note: The 108 Karanas are directly associated with the 108 names of Shiva, the 108 Upanishads, and 108 marma points of Ayurveda — establishing a unified field of sacred geometry, sound, movement, and healing.
V

Yoga: The Eternal Science

Vedic & Neurological References

Grounded in Patanjali's Yoga Sutras (196 sutras across 4 padas), the Hatha Yoga Pradipika of Swatmarama, and the Gheranda Samhita. The neurological dimension is cross-referenced with research on neuroplasticity, the vagus nerve, and the HPA axis. References modern scholars including Dr. David Frawley and Swami Satyananda Saraswati's systematic yoga research.

Contextual Note: This work argues that Patanjali's eight-limbed path (Ashtanga) constitutes a complete psychoneuroimmunological protocol — from ethical conduct (Yama/Niyama) through to the biochemistry of Samadhi.
VI

Shabda Brahman

Philosophy of Sacred Sound

Primary sources include the Sphota theory of Bhartrihari's Vakyapadiya (5th c. CE), the Mandukyopanishad's treatment of AUM, and the tantric Matrika Chakra system of 50 Sanskrit phonemes. Draws on Kashmir Shaivism's doctrine of Parashakti as primordial sound. Modern references include cymatics research and quantum field theory as analogical frameworks.

Contextual Note: Bhartrihari's central thesis — that all reality is a manifestation of the Shabda Brahman (the Supreme as Word) — parallels modern information-theoretic theories of the universe as a computational substrate.
VII

The Cosmic Choreography

Shankaracharya & Quantum Metaphysics

Central texts include Adi Shankaracharya's Brahmasutra Bhashya, Soundarya Lahari, and Manasollasa. Interprets Nataraja iconography through sacred geometry and modern physics. References Fritjof Capra's The Tao of Physics for East-West physics parallels, and the Shiva Sutras of Kashmir Shaivism for the cosmological dance metaphor.

Contextual Note: The Ananda Tandava of Shiva — the cosmic dance of creation, preservation and dissolution — is shown to be a precise metaphor for particle-antiparticle creation and annihilation in quantum field theory.
VIII

Sanskrit & AI

Linguistics, Computation & Neurology

Based on Panini's Ashtadhyayi (3,959 sutras, ~4th c. BCE) — demonstrated by NASA researcher Rick Briggs (1985) to be the most complete formal grammar ever constructed, suitable for AI. Cross-referenced with Noam Chomsky's generative grammar, neural linguistic programming research, and Sanskrit computational linguistics work from IIT Bombay and the University of Hyderabad.

Contextual Note: Sanskrit's zero-ambiguity grammar structure makes it uniquely suited not only for natural language processing but as a model for programming the human nervous system through precise phonemic patterning — connecting linguistics, neurology and AI.
Intellectual Heritage

The Guru Paramparā

The research of Naredla Rama Chandra stands at the confluence of multiple lineages of Indian knowledge. The following sacred tradition of teachers (Guru Paramparā) forms the intellectual and spiritual backbone of his enquiry — from the primordial rishis who first cognised the Vedic hymns, through the great systematisers of classical India, to the modern renaissance scholars who re-contextualised this wisdom for contemporary understanding.

🌅
The Vedic Rishis
c. 3000–1500 BCE
Seers who cognised the four Vedas: Rigveda, Samaveda (source of musical notation), Yajurveda, Atharvaveda. Originators of Nada Brahman doctrine.
📜
Bharata Muni
c. 200 BCE – 200 CE
Author of the Natya Shastra — the foundational text on Indian performing arts, codifying the 108 Karanas, the Rasa theory, and the science of Nada.
🔤
Panini
c. 4th c. BCE
Author of the Ashtadhyayi — the world's most precise linguistic grammar, foundational to the Sanskrit & AI research. Revered as the father of linguistics.
🧘
Patanjali
c. 2nd c. BCE
Compiler of the Yoga Sutras — the systematic science of consciousness and meditation. Provides the psychological framework underlying the Master Consciousness Protocol.
🌊
Bhartrihari
c. 5th c. CE
Author of Vakyapadiya — the greatest philosophical grammar in Sanskrit, establishing the doctrine of Shabda Brahman (the universe as sacred sound).
☀️
Adi Shankaracharya
788–820 CE
Consolidator of Advaita Vedanta, author of Brahmasutra Bhashya and Soundarya Lahari. His metaphysics of non-dual consciousness anchors The Cosmic Choreography.
🎶
Sarngadeva
13th c. CE
Author of Sangita Ratnakara — the definitive medieval treatise on Carnatic and Hindustani music theory. Essential reference for Sapta Swaras & Healing.
💡
Abhinavagupta
950–1020 CE
The greatest aesthetician and tantric philosopher of Kashmir Shaivism. His Abhinavabharati (commentary on Natya Shastra) and Tantraloka inform the consciousness science research.
🌺
Sri Aurobindo
1872–1950
Philosopher, yogi and author of The Life Divine and The Synthesis of Yoga. His integral vision of consciousness evolution directly informs the science of consciousness research.
🔬
Swami Satyananda
1923–2009
Founder of the Bihar School of Yoga, systematic researcher of yoga therapeutics. His clinical approach to yoga science forms the empirical backbone of the Yoga research.
